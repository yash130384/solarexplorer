{{Short description|Natural satellites of the planet Neptune}}
{{good article}}
[[File:New Webb Images Capture Rare View of Neptune’s Rings (Labeled).png|alt=Shown in this image are Neptune and some of its moons: Triton, Galatea, Naiad, Thalassa, Despina, Proteus, and Larissa|thumb|upright=1.5|An annotated [[near-infrared]] image of some of [[Neptune]]'s moons as captured by the [[James Webb Space Telescope]] in September 2022.<ref group="note">The bright blue [[Diffraction spike|diffraction star]] is [[Triton (moon)|Triton]], Neptune's largest moon; while [[Hippocamp (moon)|Hippocamp]], its smallest known regular moon, is too small to be seen.</ref>]]
There are 16 known [[Natural satellite|moons]] of the planet [[Neptune]], fourteen of which are named after [[List of water deities|water deities]] and creatures in [[Greek mythology]].<ref group="note">This is an IAU guideline that will be followed at the naming of every Neptunian moon, although two ([[S/2002 N 5]] and [[S/2021 N 1]]) have yet to receive permanent names.</ref> The largest is [[Triton (moon)|Triton]], discovered by [[William Lassell]] on 10 October 1846, 17 days after the [[discovery of Neptune]] itself. Over a century passed before the discovery of the second natural satellite, [[Nereid (moon)|Nereid]], in 1949, and another 40 years before [[Proteus (moon)|Proteus]], Neptune's second-largest moon, was discovered in 1989.

Triton is unique among moons of [[Planetary-mass object|planetary mass]] in that its orbit is [[Retrograde and prograde motion|retrograde]] to Neptune's rotation and [[Orbital inclination|inclined]] relative to Neptune's equator, which suggests that it did not form in orbit around Neptune, but was instead gravitationally captured by it. The next-largest satellite in the [[Solar System]] suspected to be captured, [[Saturn]]'s moon [[Phoebe (moon)|Phoebe]], has only 0.03% of Triton's mass. The [[capture of Triton]], probably occurring some time after Neptune formed a satellite system, was a catastrophic event for Neptune's original satellites, disrupting their orbits so that they collided to form a rubble disc. Triton is massive enough to have achieved [[hydrostatic equilibrium]] and to retain a thin atmosphere capable of forming clouds and hazes.

Inward of Triton are seven small inner moons, Neptune's only [[regular moon|regular satellites]], all with [[Retrograde and prograde motion|prograde orbits]] close to the planet's equatorial plane; some orbit among [[Rings of Neptune|Neptune's rings]]. Including the largest, Proteus, they were re-accreted from the rubble disc formed after Triton's orbit became circular. Beyond Triton, Neptune has eight outer [[irregular satellite]]s: four retrograde and four prograde. Among the prograde satellites is Nereid, the largest of the eight outer moons, which orbits much farther from Neptune at a high inclination. Its orbit is unusually close and highly eccentric for an irregular satellite, suggesting it may once have been a regular satellite significantly perturbed when Triton was captured. The outermost moons of Neptune are the furthest moons from their primaries in the Solar System.

==History==
===Discovery===
[[Triton (moon)|Triton]] was discovered by [[William Lassell]] in 1846, just seventeen days after the [[discovery of Neptune]].<ref name=Lassell1846/> [[Nereid (moon)|Nereid]] was discovered by [[Gerard Kuiper|Gerard P. Kuiper]] in 1949.<ref name=Kuiper1949/> The third moon, later named [[Larissa (moon)|Larissa]], was first observed by Harold J. Reitsema, William B. Hubbard, Larry A. Lebofsky, and [[David J. Tholen]] on 24 May 1981. The astronomers were observing a star's close approach to Neptune, looking for rings similar to [[Rings of Uranus|those discovered around Uranus]] four years earlier.<ref name="Reitsema1982" /> If rings were present, the star's luminosity would decrease slightly just before the planet's closest approach. The star's luminosity dipped only for several seconds, which meant that it was due to a moon rather than rings.

No further moons were found until ''[[Voyager 2]]'' flew by Neptune in 1989. ''Voyager 2'' recovered Larissa and discovered five inner moons: [[Naiad (moon)|Naiad]], [[Thalassa (moon)|Thalassa]], [[Despina (moon)|Despina]], [[Galatea (moon)|Galatea]] and [[Proteus (moon)|Proteus]].<ref name="Smith1989" /> In 2002 and 2003, two surveys using large ground-based telescopes found five additional outer irregular moons, bringing the total to thirteen.<ref name="HolmanKavelaarsGrav2004" /> These moons were [[Halimede (moon)|Halimede]], [[Sao (moon)|Sao]], [[Laomedeia]], [[Neso (moon)|Neso]], and [[Psamathe (moon)|Psamathe]].<ref name="HolmanKavelaarsGrav2004" /><ref name="SheppardJewittKleyna2006" /> A sixth moon was also detected in 2002, but it could not be re-observed enough times to determine its orbit, so it became [[lost asteroid|lost]].<ref name="HolmanKavelaarsGrav2004"/>

In 2013 [[Mark R. Showalter]] discovered [[Hippocamp (moon)|Hippocamp]] while examining Hubble Space Telescope images of Neptune's [[Rings of Neptune#Adams ring|ring arcs]] from 2009. He used a technique similar to [[Panning (camera)|panning]] to compensate for orbital motion and allow [[Shift-and-add|stacking]] of multiple images to bring out faint details.<ref name = "STScI_2013-07-15">{{cite web| title = Hubble Finds New Neptune Moon| publisher = [[Space Telescope Science Institute]]| date = 2013-07-15| url = http://hubblesite.org/newscenter/archive/releases/2013/30/full/| access-date = 2021-01-28| archive-date = 2016-03-04| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20160304014616/http://hubblesite.org/newscenter/archive/releases/2013/30/full/| url-status = live}}</ref><ref name = "Showalter_blog">{{cite web | last = Showalter | first = M. R. | author-link = Mark R. Showalter | title = How to Photograph a Racehorse ...and how this relates to a tiny moon of Neptune | work = Mark Showalter's blog | date = 2013-07-15 | url = http://cosmicdiary.org/mshowalter/2013/07/15/how-to-photograph-a-racehorse-and-how-this-relates-to-a-tiny-moon-of-neptune | access-date = 2013-07-16 | archive-date = 2013-07-18 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20130718052533/http://cosmicdiary.org/mshowalter/2013/07/15/how-to-photograph-a-racehorse-and-how-this-relates-to-a-tiny-moon-of-neptune/ | url-status = live }}</ref> After deciding on a whim to expand the search area to radii well beyond the rings, he found an unambiguous dot that represented the new moon.<ref name="Beatty_2013">{{cite news |url=http://www.skyandtelescope.com/astronomy-news/neptunes-newest-moon/ |title=Neptune's Newest Moon |work=[[Sky & Telescope]] |author=Kelly Beatty |date=15 July 2013 |access-date=12 June 2017 |archive-date=10 August 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140810155711/http://www.skyandtelescope.com/astronomy-news/neptunes-newest-moon/ |url-status=live }}</ref> He then found it repeatedly in other archival HST images going back to 2004. ''Voyager 2'', which had observed all of Neptune's other inner satellites, did not detect it during its 1989 flyby, due to its dimness.<ref name="STScI_2013-07-15" />

In 2021, [[Scott S. Sheppard]] and colleagues used the [[Subaru Telescope]] at [[Mauna Kea, Hawaii]] and discovered two more irregular moons of Neptune, which were announced in 2024.<ref name="Sheppardmoons2024"/> These two moons are [[provisional designation|provisionally designated]] [[S/2021 N 1]] and [[S/2002 N 5]]. The latter turned out to be a recovery of the lost moon from 2002.<ref name="MPEC-2024-D112"/><ref name="MPEC-2024-D114"/>

==Characteristics==
The moons of Neptune can be divided into two groups: [[regular satellite|regular]] and [[irregular satellite|irregular]]. The first group includes the seven inner moons, which follow circular prograde orbits lying in the equatorial plane of Neptune. The second group consists of all nine other moons including Triton. They generally follow inclined eccentric and often retrograde orbits far from Neptune; the only exception is Triton, which orbits close to the planet following a circular orbit, though retrograde and inclined.<ref name="Jewitt2007" />

===Regular moons===
In order of distance from Neptune, the regular moons are [[Naiad (moon)|Naiad]], [[Thalassa (moon)|Thalassa]], [[Despina (moon)|Despina]], [[Galatea (moon)|Galatea]], [[Larissa (moon)|Larissa]], [[Hippocamp (moon)|Hippocamp]], and [[Proteus (moon)|Proteus]]. All but the outer two are within Neptune-[[synchronous orbit]] (Neptune's rotational period is 0.6713 day or 16 hours<ref name="fact">{{cite web |last = Williams |first = David R. |date = 1 September 2004 |url = http://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/neptunefact.html |title = Neptune Fact Sheet |publisher = NASA |access-date = 18 July 2013 |archive-date = 1 July 2010 |archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20100701192119/http://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/neptunefact.html |url-status = live }}</ref>) and thus are being [[Tidal deceleration|tidally decelerated]]. Naiad, the closest regular moon, is also the second smallest among the inner moons (following the discovery of Hippocamp), whereas Proteus is the largest regular moon and the second largest moon of Neptune. The first five moons orbit much faster than Neptune's rotation itself ranging from 7 hours for Naiad and Thalassa, to 13 hours for Larissa.

The inner moons are closely associated with [[Rings of Neptune|Neptune's rings]]. The two innermost satellites, Naiad and Thalassa, orbit between the [[Rings of Neptune#Galle ring|Galle]] and [[Rings of Neptune#Le Verrier ring|LeVerrier rings]].<ref name="Smith1989" /> Despina may be a [[shepherd moon]] of the LeVerrier ring, because its orbit lies just inside this ring.<ref name=Miner2007b/> The next moon, [[Galatea (moon)|Galatea]], orbits just inside the most prominent of Neptune's rings, the [[Adams ring]].<ref name=Miner2007b/> This ring is very narrow, with a width not exceeding 50&nbsp;km,<ref name=Horn1990/> and has five embedded bright [[arc (geometry)|arcs]].<ref name=Miner2007b/> The gravity of Galatea helps confine the ring particles within a limited region in the radial direction, maintaining the narrow ring. Various [[orbital resonance|resonances]] between the ring particles and Galatea may also have a role in maintaining the arcs.<ref name=Miner2007b/>

Only the two largest regular moons have been imaged with a resolution sufficient to discern their shapes and surface features.<ref name="Smith1989" /> Larissa, about 200&nbsp;km in diameter, is slightly elongated.<ref name="Stooke1994"/> Proteus is also not significantly elongated, but not fully spherical either: it resembles an irregular [[polyhedron]], with several flat or slightly [[Curved mirror#Concave mirrors|concave]] facets 150 to 250&nbsp;km in diameter.<ref name="Stooke1994" /> At about 400&nbsp;km in diameter, it is larger than the Saturnian moon [[Mimas (moon)|Mimas]], which is fully ellipsoidal. This difference may be due to a past collisional disruption of Proteus.<ref name=BanfieldMurray/> The surface of Proteus is heavily cratered and shows a number of linear features. Its largest crater, Pharos, is more than 150&nbsp;km in diameter.<ref name="Smith1989" /><ref name="Stooke1994" />

All of Neptune's inner moons are dark objects: their [[geometric albedo]] ranges from 7 to 10%.<ref name=Karkoschka2003/> Their [[spectrum|spectra]] indicate that they are made from water ice contaminated by some very dark material. In this respect, the inner Neptunian moons are similar to the inner [[moons of Uranus|Uranian moons]].<ref name="Smith1989" />

===Irregular moons===
[[File:Irregular moon orbits a vs. i.jpg|thumb|upright=1.5|Irregular satellites of all four giant planets, plotted by average distance from their planet ([[semi-major axis]] in [[Hill radius|Hill radii]]) and [[orbital inclination]] ([[degree (angle)|degrees]] with respect to [[ecliptic]]). Irregular moons of Neptune are plotted as blue diamonds. Data as of February 2024.]]

In order of their distance from the planet, the irregular moons are [[Triton (moon)|Triton]], [[Nereid (moon)|Nereid]], [[Halimede (moon)|Halimede]], [[Sao (moon)|Sao]], [[S/2002 N 5]], [[Laomedeia]], [[Psamathe (moon)|Psamathe]], [[Neso (moon)|Neso]], and [[S/2021 N 1]], a group that includes both prograde and retrograde objects.<ref name="Jewitt2007" /> The seven outermost moons are similar to the irregular moons of other [[giant planet]]s, and are thought to have been gravitationally captured by Neptune, unlike the regular satellites, which probably formed ''[[in situ]]''.<ref name="SheppardJewittKleyna2006" />

Triton and Nereid are unusual irregular satellites and are thus treated separately from the other seven irregular Neptunian moons, which are more like the outer irregular satellites of the other outer planets.<ref name="SheppardJewittKleyna2006" /><ref name="Nicholson2008"/> Firstly, they are the largest two known irregular moons in the Solar System, with Triton being about eight times larger than Nereid in terms of diameter.<ref name="SheppardJewittKleyna2006" /> Secondly, they both have atypically small semi-major axes,<ref name="Belyakov2026"/> with Triton's being about twelve times smaller than the runner-up ([[Francisco (moon)|Francisco]] around Uranus, with the third place being Nereid).<ref name="jplsats"/> Thirdly, they both have unusual orbital eccentricities: Nereid has one of the most eccentric orbits of any known irregular satellite, and Triton's orbit is a nearly perfect circle.<ref name="Nicholson2008"/> Finally, Nereid also has the lowest inclination of any known irregular satellite.<ref name="SheppardJewittKleyna2006" />

====Triton====
{{Main|Triton (moon)}}

Triton follows a retrograde and quasi-circular orbit, and is thought to be a gravitationally captured satellite. It was the second moon in the Solar System that was discovered to have a substantial [[atmosphere]], which is primarily [[nitrogen]] with small amounts of [[methane]] and [[carbon monoxide]].<ref name="ElliotStrobelZhu_2000" /> The pressure on Triton's surface is about 14&nbsp;[[bar (unit)|μbar]].<ref name="ElliotStrobelZhu_2000" /> In 1989, the ''Voyager 2'' spacecraft observed what appeared to be clouds and hazes in this thin atmosphere.<ref name="Smith1989" /> Triton is one of the coldest bodies in the Solar System, with a surface temperature of about {{convert|38|K|C}}.<ref name="ElliotStrobelZhu_2000" /> Its surface is covered by nitrogen, methane, [[carbon dioxide]] and water [[Volatile (astrogeology)|ices]]<ref name=Cruikshank1993/> and has a high [[geometric albedo]] of more than 70%.<ref name="Smith1989" /> The [[Bond albedo]] is even higher, reaching up to 90%.<ref name="Smith1989" /><ref group="note">The geometric albedo of an astronomical body is the ratio of its actual brightness at zero [[Phase angle (astronomy)|phase angle]] (i.e. as seen from the light source) to that of an idealized flat, fully reflecting, [[diffuse reflection|diffusively scattering]] ([[Lambertian reflectance|Lambertian]]) disk with the same cross-section. The Bond albedo, named after the American astronomer [[George Phillips Bond]] (1825–1865), who originally proposed it, is the fraction of [[power (physics)|power]] in the total [[electromagnetic radiation]] incident on an astronomical body that is scattered back out into space. The Bond albedo is a value strictly between 0 and 1, as it includes all possible scattered light (but not radiation from the body itself).  This is in contrast to other definitions of [[albedo]] such as the geometric albedo, which can be above 1. In general, though, the Bond albedo may be greater or smaller than the geometric albedo, depending on surface and atmospheric properties of the body in question.</ref> Surface features include the large southern [[polar ice cap|polar cap]], older cratered planes cross-cut by [[graben]] and [[Escarpment|scarps]], as well as youthful features probably formed by endogenic processes like [[cryovolcano|cryovolcanism]].<ref name="Smith1989" /> ''Voyager 2'' observations revealed a number of active [[geyser]]s within the polar cap heated by the Sun, which eject plumes up to 8&nbsp;km high.<ref name="Smith1989" /> Triton has a relatively high density of about 2&nbsp;g/cm<sup>3</sup>, indicating that rocks constitute about two thirds of its mass, and ices (mainly water ice) the remaining one third. There may be a layer of liquid water deep inside Triton, forming a [[subsurface ocean]].<ref name=Hussmann2006/> Because of its retrograde orbit and relative proximity to Neptune (closer than the Moon is to Earth), [[tidal deceleration]] is causing Triton to spiral inward, which will lead to its destruction in about 28&nbsp;billion years.<ref name="Wang2025">{{cite journal |last1=Wang |first1=K. |last2=Zhang |first2=H. |last3=Li |first3=Y. |last4=Qiao |first4=L. |title=A Plausible Minimum Value of the Neptunian Tidal Dissipation Factor Estimated from Triton’s Astrometric Observations |journal=Solar System Research |volume=59 |issue=1 |pages=6–15 |doi=10.1134/S0038094624601440 |url=https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S0038094624601440 |date=2025-02-18 |access-date=2026-05-12|url-access=subscription }}</ref>

====Nereid====
{{Main|Nereid (moon)}}

Nereid is the third-largest moon of Neptune. It has a prograde, but very eccentric, orbit and is believed to be a former regular satellite that was scattered to its current orbit through gravitational interactions during Triton's capture.<ref name="Goldreich1989" /> Water ice has been spectroscopically detected on its surface. Early measurements of Nereid showed large, irregular variations in its visible magnitude, which were speculated to be caused by forced [[precession]] or chaotic rotation combined with an elongated shape and bright or dark spots on the surface.<ref name=Shaefer2008/> This was disproved in 2016, when observations from the [[Kepler space telescope]] showed only minor variations. Thermal modeling based on infrared observations from the [[Spitzer Space Telescope|Spitzer]] and [[Herschel Space Observatory|Herschel]] space telescopes suggest that Nereid is only moderately elongated, which disfavours forced precession of the rotation.<ref name="Kiss2016" /> The thermal model also indicates that the surface roughness of Nereid is very high, likely similar to the Saturnian moon [[Hyperion (moon)|Hyperion]].<ref name="Kiss2016" />

Nereid dominates the normal irregular satellites of Neptune, having about 98% of the mass of Neptune's entire irregular satellite system altogether (if Triton is not counted). This is similar to the situation of [[Phoebe (moon)|Phoebe]] at Saturn. It is also larger by far than all normal irregular satellites, having about two-thirds the mass of all non-Triton irregular moons combined.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://tilmanndenk.de/outersaturnianmoons/ |title=Outer Moons of Saturn |last=Denk |first=Tilmann |date=2024 |website=tilmanndenk.de |publisher=Tilmann Denk |access-date=25 February 2024 |quote= |archive-date=24 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240224094854/https://tilmanndenk.de/outersaturnianmoons/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Spectroscopic observations conducted by the [[James Webb Space Telescope]] have shown that Nereid's spectrum differs significantly from those of [[minor planet]]s in the outer Solar System. Although many irregular satellites share similarities with Kuiper belt objects, Nereid does not correspond to the observed [[Trans-Neptunian spectral types|spectral types of Kuiper belt objects]], implying that it likely formed around Neptune instead of being captured. If this interpretation is confirmed, Nereid would represent the only intact original moon of Neptune to have survived Triton's destructive arrival.<ref name="Belyakov2026">{{Cite journal |last=Belyakov |first=Matthew |last2=Davis |first2=M. Ryleigh |last3=Wong |first3=Ian |last4=Batygin |first4=Konstantin |author-link4=Konstantin Batygin |last5=Brown |first5=Michael E. |author-link5=Michael E. Brown |title=Nereid as a regular satellite of Neptune |url=https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aeb1429 |journal=Science Advances |volume=21 |issue=11 |doi=10.1126/sciadv.aeb1429 |date=May 2026|arxiv=2606.02818 }}</ref>

====Normal irregular moons====
Among the remaining irregular moons, Sao, S/2002 N 5, and Laomedeia follow prograde orbits, whereas Halimede, Psamathe, Neso and S/2021 N 1 follow retrograde orbits. There are at least two groups of moons that share similar orbits, with the prograde moons Sao, S/2002 N 5, and Laomedeia belonging to the Sao group and the retrograde moons Psamathe, Neso, and S/2021 N 1 belonging to the Neso group.<ref name="Sheppardmoons2024"/> The moons of the Neso group have the largest orbits of any natural satellites discovered in the Solar System to date, with average orbital distances over 125 times the distance between Earth and the Moon and orbital periods over 25 years.<ref name="sheppardmoons"/> In particular, Neptune's outermost moon, [[S/2021 N 1]], which has an orbital period of about 27 [[Earth years]], orbits farther from its planet than any other known moon in the [[Solar System]].<ref name="jplsats"/><ref name="MPEC-2024-D112"/> Neptune has the largest [[Hill sphere]] in the Solar System, owing primarily to its large distance from the Sun; this allows it to retain control of such distant moons.<ref name="Jewitt2007" /> Nevertheless, the Jovian moons in the [[Carme group|Carme]] and [[Pasiphae group]]s orbit at a greater percentage of their primary's Hill radius than the Neso group moons.<ref name="Jewitt2007" />

==List of moons==

The Neptunian moons are listed here by orbital period, from shortest to longest. Irregular (captured) moons are marked by color and symbols, distinguishing the Sao and Neso groups, as well as Triton, Nereid, and Halimede which do not fit in either. The orbits and mean distances of the irregular moons are variable over short timescales due to frequent planetary and solar [[perturbation (astronomy)|perturbations]]; therefore, the listed orbital elements of all irregular moons are averaged over a 30,000-year period: these may differ from [[osculating orbit|osculating]] orbital elements provided by other sources.<ref name="Brozovic2022"/> The orbital elements of Triton and Nereid are based on the [[Epoch (astronomy)|epoch]] of 1 January 2000,<ref name="Yuan2021"/> while those of the other moons are all based on the epoch of 1 January 2020.<ref name="Brozovic2020"/><ref name="jplsats">{{cite web|title=Planetary Satellite Mean Elements|url=https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/|publisher=Jet Propulsion Laboratory|accessdate=9 October 2024|archive-date=9 October 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241009025136/https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/|url-status=live}}</ref> Triton, the only Neptunian moon massive enough for its surface to have [[gravitational collapse|collapsed]] into a [[spheroid]], is emboldened.

==Formation==
{{Further|Capture of Triton}}
The mass distribution of the Neptunian moons is the most lopsided of the satellite systems of the [[giant planet]]s in the Solar System. One moon, Triton, makes up nearly all of the mass of the system, with all other moons together comprising only one third of one percent. This is similar to the moon system of Saturn, where [[Titan (moon)|Titan]] makes up more than 95% of the total mass, but is different from the more balanced systems of Jupiter and Uranus. The reason for the lopsidedness of the present Neptunian system is that Triton was captured well after the formation of Neptune's original satellite system, and experts conjecture much of the system was destroyed in the process of capture.<ref name="Goldreich1989" /><ref name=Naeye2006>{{cite journal|journal=[[Sky & Telescope]]|date=September 2006|title=Triton Kidnap Caper|volume=112|first=R.|issue=3|last=Naeye|page=18|bibcode=2006S&T...112c..18N}}</ref>

[[File:Masa de triton.svg|thumb|The relative masses of the Neptunian moons]]
Triton's orbit upon capture would have been highly eccentric, and would have caused chaotic perturbations in the orbits of the original inner Neptunian satellites, causing them to collide and reduce to a disc of rubble.<ref name="Goldreich1989" /> This means it is likely that Neptune's present inner satellites are not the original bodies that formed with Neptune. Only after Triton's orbit became circularised could some of the rubble re-accrete into the present-day regular moons.<ref name="BanfieldMurray" /> These satellites were probably destroyed again by later impacts from [[Comet|comets]], and so reformed even more recently than Triton's capture event, with the exception of Proteus which was likely large enough to survive the impacts.<ref name="BanfieldMurray" />

The mechanism of Triton's capture has been the subject of several theories over the years. One of them postulates that Triton was captured in a [[three-body problem|three-body]] encounter. In this scenario, Triton is the surviving member of a [[Binary asteroid|binary]] [[Kuiper belt]] object{{refn|group=note|[[Minor-planet moon#Trans-Neptunian objects|Binary objects]], objects with moons such as the [[Pluto]]–[[Charon (moon)|Charon]] system, are quite common among the larger [[trans-Neptunian object]]s (TNOs). Around 11% of all TNOs may be binaries.<ref name="Agnor2006" />}} disrupted by its encounter with Neptune.<ref name="Agnor2006" />

Numerical simulations show that there is a 41% probability that the moon Halimede collided with Nereid at some time in the past.<ref name="HolmanKavelaarsGrav2004" /> Although it is not known whether any collision has taken place, both moons appear to have similar ("grey") colors, implying that Halimede could be a fragment of Nereid.<ref name="GravHolmanFraser2004">{{cite journal| doi = 10.1086/424997| last1 = Grav| first1 = Tommy| last2 = Holman| first2 = Matthew J.| author-link2 = Matthew J. Holman| last3 = Fraser| first3 = Wesley C.| date = 2004-09-20| title = Photometry of Irregular Satellites of Uranus and Neptune| journal = [[The Astrophysical Journal]]| volume = 613| issue = 1| pages = L77–L80| arxiv = astro-ph/0405605| bibcode = 2004ApJ...613L..77G| s2cid = 15706906}}</ref>
